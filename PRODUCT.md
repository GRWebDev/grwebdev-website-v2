# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

GRWebDev primarily serves learners and newcomers who want a welcoming way into the Grand Rapids web community. The audience also includes working developers, tech enthusiasts, students, creatives, speakers, sponsors, organizers, and community partners who want to meet people, share knowledge, and support local web work.

Visitors are often trying to answer practical questions quickly: what is happening next, whether they will feel welcome there, where to go, who is involved, and how to support the group.

## Product Purpose

GRWebDev exists to open friendly, approachable spaces where people can learn, present, discuss web development, and connect with the local community.

The website should make events easy to discover, make the group feel human and accessible, explain the organization clearly, and give sponsors or supporters a confident path to participate. Success looks like more people feeling comfortable showing up, returning, speaking, volunteering, or sponsoring.

## Brand Personality

Community. Belonging. Space.

The brand should feel welcoming, local, inclusive, practical, and alive. It should make technical topics feel approachable without flattening the group into generic meetup branding. The voice should be direct, warm, and lightly informal, with enough confidence to feel organized and trustworthy.

## Anti-references

The site should not feel corporate. Avoid polished enterprise sameness, stock-photo professionalism, investor-deck language, generic SaaS sections, sterile tech conference aesthetics, and anything that makes newcomers feel like they need credentials before they belong.

Avoid visual and copy patterns that imply exclusivity, insider status, hustle culture, or a narrow definition of who counts as a developer.

## Design Principles

Show people the room before asking them to enter.

Make the next step obvious, whether that step is attending an event, finding a location, learning about the board, or supporting the group.

Keep the local community visible. Sponsors, board members, venues, event flyers, and real names should feel like part of the brand, not administrative extras.

Let technical confidence coexist with warmth. The site can be structured and precise while still feeling open to beginners.

Prefer plain language over institutional language. The site should sound like people inviting people, not a committee publishing a brochure.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support reduced motion preferences, keyboard-friendly navigation, visible focus states, semantic page structure, and color contrast that does not rely on red and black alone.

Design decisions should consider newcomers, people attending alone, people with different levels of technical confidence, and people who need clear logistical information before choosing to show up.
